This Markdown file is structured for use as a plant-care knowledge base in an app.

## Core watering rule

**Check daily; do not automatically water daily.**

Water according to the moisture trigger for the species. When watering, soak the entire rootball until water exits the drainage holes, then allow the pot to drain completely.

# Identification and taxonomy notes

## Chinese Fringe Tree

**Assumed ID:** *Chionanthus retusus*. Do not confuse it with **Chinese Fringe Flower, *Loropetalum chinense*** (burgundy/purple foliage, pink ribbon-like flowers). **ID confidence:** High if the nursery label literally says "Chinese Fringe Tree".

## Dragon Pine

"Dragon Pine" is not a true pine. The ornamental Dragon Juniper is normally **_Juniperus chinensis_ 'Kaizuka'** (Dragon Juniper, Hollywood Juniper, Torulosa Juniper, Kaizuka Juniper, 龍柏). **ID confidence:** High if branches naturally twist upward in a dragon-like form.

## Orange Jasmine

**_Murraya paniculata_** is the normal botanical match for Orange Jasmine / Orange Jessamine. **ID confidence:** High.

## Triangle Fig

The horticultural name **_Ficus triangularis_ Warb.** is still widely used. Kew currently treats it as a synonym of **_Ficus natalensis_ subsp. _leprieurii_ (Miq.) C.C.Berg**. Keep both names indexed. **ID confidence:** High if leaves are distinctly triangular.

## Red Maple

This file assumes true North American Red Maple: **_Acer rubrum_ L.** Do not confuse with a red-leaved Japanese Maple cultivar (*Acer palmatum*). *Acer rubrum*: usually 3–5 lobes, less deeply divided. *Acer palmatum*: usually 5–7+ deeply cut hand-like lobes. **ID confidence:** Medium until leaf ID is confirmed.

## Dok Khem

Thai **ต้นเข็ม / ดอกเข็ม** normally refers to Ixora. Standard app species: **_Ixora coccinea_ L.** **ID confidence:** High.

## Orange Tree

This file assumes a sweet orange: **_Citrus × sinensis_ (L.) Osbeck**. It could instead be calamondin, mandarin, kumquat or another Citrus hybrid if the fruit/label differs. **ID confidence:** Medium until fruit or nursery label is confirmed.

# 1. Chinese Fringe Tree

- **scientific_name:** *Chionanthus retusus* Lindl. & Paxton — **family:** Oleaceae — deciduous shrub / small tree, temperate, dormancy required, tropical fit low–medium.
- **Origin:** eastern Asia (China, Korea, Japan, Taiwan).
- **Appearance:** deciduous leathery leaves, attractive bark with age, white spring flowers with narrow fringe-like petals, female trees can form dark blue-black olive-like fruits.
- **Light:** full sun to partial shade; bonsai target 4–6+ hours direct sun; bright shade after repotting; afternoon protection in extreme tropical heat.
- **Water:** moist. Water when top 1–2 cm begins to feel slightly dry; never bone dry. Soak fully, drain fully. Reduce in leafless dormancy but never fully desiccate roots.
- **Soil:** drainage + aeration + moderate retention; akadama/pumice/lava + small organic fraction. pH ~5.5–7.0.
- **Tropical warning:** in a permanently warm climate dormancy may be inadequate, flowering unreliable, vigour declining. Keep outside with airflow; do not treat as a tropical evergreen.
- **Fertilizer:** balanced feed every 2–4 weeks in active growth, or slow-release/organic per label.
- **Pruning:** structural work in dormancy or after flowering; pruning before bloom removes flower buds; clip-and-grow for ramification.
- **Wiring:** possible on younger branches; check frequently.
- **Repotting:** every 2–3 years young; late winter / early spring before bud burst.
- **Propagation:** seed, cuttings, layering. **Pests:** mites, scale, aphids. **Diseases:** fungal leaf spots, mildew.
- **Styles:** informal upright, broom, twin trunk, clump.
- **Daily rule:** check moisture daily in warm weather; water when top layer begins to dry.

# 2. Dragon Pine / Dragon Juniper

- **scientific_name:** *Juniperus chinensis* L. 'Kaizuka' — **family:** Cupressaceae — evergreen conifer, not a true pine, temperate to warm-temperate, tropical fit medium.
- **Identity:** twisting branches, sculptural upright growth, scale-like adult foliage, occasional needle-like juvenile foliage.
- **Light:** OUTDOOR FULL SUN, 6+ hours; never a permanent indoor bonsai.
- **Water:** let the upper substrate begin drying before watering again; water fully to runoff. Overwatering is the major risk — a juniper can stay visually green for some time after serious root damage.
- **Soil:** very airy and fast draining (pumice, lava, akadama, coarse inorganic). pH ~5.5–7.5.
- **Tropical warning:** hot roots, wet rainy season, fungal pressure, insufficient seasonal rest. Do not solve heat stress by keeping soil permanently wet.
- **Fertilizer:** balanced, regular but not excessive; development trees fed more strongly.
- **Foliage:** do not hedge-shear repeatedly; do not pinch every tip; NEVER remove all green foliage from a branch you want to keep — old bare juniper wood back-buds unreliably. Juvenile needle foliage after stress is normal.
- **Wiring:** excellent species; protect bark, inspect regularly. Heavy bends only on strong trees.
- **Deadwood:** jin/shari suitable, but health before deadwood on young material.
- **Repotting:** every 2–3 years young; spring; avoid complete bare-rooting.
- **Propagation:** cuttings, layering. **Pests:** spider mites, scale, aphids. **Diseases:** Phomopsis/Kabatina tip blight, root rot.
- **Styles:** informal upright, literati, slanting, cascade, semi-cascade, windswept, deadwood styles.
- **Daily rule:** outdoor full sun; check moisture daily; water after upper substrate begins drying.

# 3. Orange Jasmine

- **scientific_name:** *Murraya paniculata* (L.) Jack (syn. *Murraya exotica*) — **family:** Rutaceae — evergreen tropical shrub/small tree, tropical fit excellent.
- **Origin:** tropical and subtropical Asia into the Pacific.
- **Appearance:** glossy compound leaves, fragrant white flowers, red berries, dense branching, attractive trunk with age.
- **Light:** full sun to partial shade; several hours direct sun for compact growth and flowers.
- **Water:** let upper 1–2 cm begin drying, then water thoroughly. Avoid constant saturation, severe drought and surface-only watering.
- **Soil:** adaptable, good drainage (pumice, lava, akadama, moderate organic in hot climates). pH ~5.5–7.0.
- **Fertilizer:** regular balanced or citrus-type complete feed; micronutrients if chlorosis appears.
- **Pruning:** clip-and-grow; prune after flowering if flowers matter.
- **Wiring:** young branches flexible; check often — tropical growth thickens fast.
- **Repotting:** 1–2 years young if rootbound; 2–3 established; warm active growth.
- **Propagation:** seed, cuttings, air layering. **Pests:** scale, mealybugs, aphids, spider mites, whiteflies.
- **Styles:** informal upright, broom, slanting, multi-trunk, flowering bonsai.
- **Daily rule:** check daily in hot weather; water when upper substrate starts drying.

# 4. Triangle Fig

- **accepted_name:** *Ficus natalensis* subsp. *leprieurii* (Miq.) C.C.Berg — **horticultural_synonym:** *Ficus triangularis* Warb. — **family:** Moraceae — tropical evergreen Ficus, tropical fit excellent.
- **Appearance:** distinctly triangular leaves, woody trunk with age, aerial roots possible, milky latex, small figs can occur.
- **Light:** bright outdoor light, morning sun, several hours direct sun after acclimation. Too little light: sparse growth, longer internodes, leaf drop.
- **Water:** water when the upper substrate begins to feel slightly dry; not permanently wet, no repeated severe drought. Leaf drop after moves/root work — inspect soil before adding water.
- **Soil:** airy tropical mix (pumice, lava, akadama, small organic). pH ~5.5–7.0.
- **Fertilizer:** regular balanced feed; stronger during trunk development.
- **Pruning:** clip-and-grow; do NOT make full defoliation a default — selective leaf removal on strong trees only.
- **Wiring:** possible during active growth; Ficus thickens quickly around wire.
- **Repotting:** 1–2 years young, 2–3+ mature; warm active growth.
- **Propagation:** stem cuttings, air layering. **Pests:** scale, mealybugs, spider mites, thrips, whitefly.
- **Safety:** latex can irritate skin/eyes; wash hands after heavy pruning.
- **Styles:** informal upright, banyan, clump, root-over-rock, multi-trunk, broom.
- **Daily rule:** check daily in heat; water when upper layer starts to dry.

# 5. Red Maple

- **scientific_name:** *Acer rubrum* L. — **family:** Sapindaceae — deciduous broadleaf, temperate, dormancy required, tropical fit LOW, ID confidence medium.
- **Origin:** eastern and central North America.
- **Appearance:** opposite leaves, usually 3–5 lobes, red flowers, red samaras, orange-red autumn colour, thin young bark.
- **Light:** sun to partial shade; in extreme heat strong morning sun + afternoon shade; protect roots from overheating.
- **Water:** moist. During leaf growth water before the rootball dries; in dormancy water less but never desiccate.
- **Soil:** retention + drainage + oxygen (akadama, pumice, lava, optional organic). pH ~5.0–6.8; alkaline soil causes chlorosis.
- **Tropical warning — HIGH priority:** inadequate winter rest, leaf scorch, root overheating, weak seasonal reset, gradual decline. Cool highland far better than hot lowland.
- **Fertilizer:** balanced moderate; avoid excess nitrogen on refined trees.
- **Pruning:** structural while dormant; thin bark marks easily; sharp clean tools.
- **Wiring:** possible; scars easily; check frequently.
- **Repotting:** every 1–2 years young; early spring at bud swell.
- **Leaf scorch causes:** hot dry wind, afternoon sun, root heat, drought, fertilizer salts.
- **Propagation:** seed, cuttings, air layering. **Pests:** aphids, scale, caterpillars, mites. **Diseases:** Verticillium wilt, leaf spots.
- **Styles:** informal upright, broom, clump, forest.
- **Daily rule:** during leaf growth keep rootball moist and check daily; never let a small pot bake dry.

# 6. Dok Khem / Ixora

- **scientific_name:** *Ixora coccinea* L. — **thai_names:** เข็ม, ดอกเข็ม — **family:** Rubiaceae — wet tropical evergreen shrub, tropical fit excellent.
- **Origin:** South Asia through Indochina; Thailand within native range.
- **Appearance:** dense evergreen shrub, opposite glossy leaves, clusters of tubular flowers (red/orange/pink/yellow/white by cultivar), naturally compact.
- **Light:** strong light, full sun after acclimation; too much shade = fewer flowers, loose growth.
- **Water:** water when upper 1–2 cm begins drying; avoid complete drought and waterlogging.
- **Soil — key point:** ACIDIC, well-drained, airy with some retention. pH ~5.0–6.5. Alkaline substrate/water causes chlorosis (yellow leaves, green veins) — do not just add nitrogen.
- **Fertilizer:** balanced + micronutrients; acid-loving fertilizer if pH is high.
- **Flowering/pruning rule:** flowers form on shoot tips — hard pruning removes them. Prune after a flowering flush, then let selected tips mature.
- **Wiring:** young branches only; older branches brittle.
- **Repotting:** 1–2 years young, 2–3 established; warm active growth; no aggressive bare-rooting.
- **Propagation:** stem cuttings (also layering, seed). **Pests:** scale, aphids, mealybugs, spider mites. **Diseases:** root rot, leaf spots, sooty mould.
- **Styles:** informal upright, broom, clump, flowering bonsai, shohin.
- **Daily rule:** check daily in heat; water when upper layer begins to dry; keep substrate acidic and airy.

# 7. Sweet Orange / Orange Tree

- **horticultural_name:** *Citrus × sinensis* (L.) Osbeck — **family:** Rutaceae — evergreen subtropical/tropical fruit tree, tropical fit excellent, ID confidence medium.
- **Appearance:** evergreen aromatic foliage, fragrant white flowers, orange fruit, some thorns, often grafted.
- **Light:** FULL SUN, 6–8+ hours.
- **Water:** water when the upper layer begins to dry while the inner rootball still holds some moisture; deep watering to runoff; no standing water, no repeated complete drought.
- **Soil:** fertile, well-drained, aerated (pumice, lava, akadama, moderate organic). pH ~5.5–6.5.
- **Fertilizer:** HEAVY feeder — complete citrus nutrition with N-P-K plus magnesium, iron, manganese, zinc and other micronutrients.
- **Graft warning:** locate the graft union; remove rootstock suckers emerging below it.
- **Fruit:** uses much energy — thin excess fruit on young/small/weak trees.
- **Pruning:** remove dead wood, crossing branches, inward growth, suckers; clip-and-grow for refinement.
- **Wiring:** young branches; Citrus can be brittle and thorny; wire scars growing branches.
- **Repotting:** ~2–3 years; warm active growth.
- **Propagation:** seed (not true to type), cuttings, budding, grafting, air layering.
- **Pests:** aphids, scale, mealybugs, spider mites, citrus leafminer, citrus psyllids, caterpillars.
- **Diseases:** root rot/Phytophthora, citrus canker, HLB (citrus greening — blotchy yellowing, weak shoots, misshapen fruit: isolate and identify).
- **Styles:** informal upright, slanting, fruiting naturalistic form.
- **Daily rule:** full sun; check pot daily in heat; water when the upper layer starts drying.

## Group A — keep more consistently moist
Red Maple · Chinese Fringe Tree · Dok Khem / Ixora

## Group B — moderate moisture
Orange Jasmine · Triangle Fig · Sweet Orange

## Group C — more drying / maximum root oxygen
Dragon Juniper
Allow upper substrate to dry more clearly; water thoroughly; never permanently wet.

# Suggested reminder logic for the app

The app should normally say **"Check soil moisture"** instead of **"Water plant"**, because actual frequency changes with temperature, humidity, sun, wind, rain, pot size, substrate and root density.

- **Hot sunny weather:** check all bonsai every morning; very small pots again late afternoon in exceptional heat/wind.
- **Cool/cloudy weather:** check daily; watering frequency may drop dramatically.
- **Rainy weather:** prioritize drainage, saturation and fungal inspection; do not automatically add water.
